# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be decent. Assume the other person is trying to build something good.

## What that means here

- **Critique the work, never the person.** "This traversal is O(n²) on a dense
  graph, here is the case" is useful. "This is amateur" is not.
- **Bring evidence.** A benchmark, a failing test, a spec paragraph, a line
  number. Strong opinions are welcome when they arrive with something checkable.
- **Assume good faith on the first read**, and ask before escalating. Text loses
  tone, and most people are not being hostile — they are being brief.
- **Disagree, then commit.** Once a decision is recorded in an ADR, argue with the
  ADR rather than re-litigating it in every review.
- **A lot of the code here is written by AI agents.** Review it exactly as you
  would review a human's: on the merits. Neither "an AI wrote it" nor "a human
  wrote it" is an argument.

## Not acceptable

Harassment, personal attacks, discriminatory language, sustained disruption,
publishing someone's private information, or unwelcome attention of any kind.

## Enforcement

Report to the maintainers privately — GitHub's private report flow, or a direct
message to a maintainer. Reports are handled confidentially. Maintainers may warn,
remove content, or ban, in proportion to the behaviour.

Maintainers are held to this at least as strictly as everyone else.
